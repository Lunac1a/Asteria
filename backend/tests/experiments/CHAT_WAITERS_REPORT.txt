Installed Starlette concurrency.py:32 delegates synchronous work to AnyIO;

Untuned defaults: CHAT_SINGLEFLIGHT_PER_REQUEST_LIMIT=4 and
CHAT_SINGLEFLIGHT_GLOBAL_LIMIT=8. These are below the local HTTP concurrency and
default thread limits, and prevent one flight consuming the entire follower budget.
They do not reserve capacity for unrelated requests or establish optimal sizing.
Nonnegative configuration is validated; zero disables new follower admission.
The process registry reads limits at construction, so changes require restart.

app/services/chat/singleflight.py: one lock checks parameter conflict then both
limits and reserves both counters atomically. Matching overflow returns 429;
parameter conflicts still return 409 even at capacity. Every accepted follower
has its own reservation referencing its exact flight. The request-ID budget
spans generations until old followers finish cleanup, so a new
leader cannot bypass their occupied per-request budget. release() under the same
lock is idempotent, including concurrent duplicate cleanup. Leader completion
does not reset follower counters: waking followers release their own reservations.
app/api/routes/chat.py: finally releases both counters after auth/read rollback
and waiting, including exceptions or timeout, before reading the persisted result.
Waiting never holds the registry lock. 504 and persisted replay semantics remain.
chat_slot stays 1; no queue, retry, Session lock, Worker or Recovery changes.

- Old reservations do not decrement a replacement flight's counters.
- Route rollback/wait/replay exceptions leave no follower capacity allocated.

Limitations
Process-local capacity only. It bounds admitted waiting followers, not requests
before authentication, leader attempts, HTTP connections or replay reads after
wait completion. Client disconnect does not necessarily stop a synchronous route;
its capacity is retained until wait completion or bounded timeout. There is no
fairness/queue guarantee. The global cross-ID bound is verified at the Registry
level; HTTP tests preserve the existing single Chat leader policy. No stress test,
multi-worker support or optimal capacity claim is made.
